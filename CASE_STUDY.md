# Hypothetical Case Study: Skin Rash After Starting a Medicine

## Case summary

An adult patient is prescribed amoxicillin for a suspected bacterial
infection. A skin rash is reported two days after treatment begins. The
treating clinician stops the medicine, and the rash improves over the
following days. The available case notes do not identify another likely
cause. The medicine is not restarted.

**This scenario is fictional and intended only to demonstrate the
assessment process.** A real case would require a complete medical
history and clinical review.

## Key information

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Assessment point                    Information in this hypothetical
                                      case
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Suspected medicine                  Amoxicillin

  Reported event                      Skin rash

  Time to onset                       Two days after starting treatment

  Dechallenge                         Rash improved after the medicine
                                      was stopped

  Alternative causes                  None identified in the limited
                                      fictional notes; a real assessment
                                      must investigate them

  Rechallenge                         Not performed; it is not required
                                      for the Probable/Likely category
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

## WHO--UMC assessment

**Provisional classification: Probable/Likely**, assuming the timeline
is clinically reasonable, alternative causes have been adequately
considered and are unlikely, and improvement after withdrawal is judged
clinically consistent.

### Rationale

1.  The event occurred after the medicine was started, with a plausible
    time relationship in this illustrative scenario.
2.  The event improved after withdrawal, which supports a possible
    relationship.
3.  No alternative cause is identified in the simplified scenario. In a
    real case, other medicines, infection-related rash, allergy history,
    and other clinical explanations would need review.
4.  Rechallenge was not performed. Rechallenge is not required for the
    WHO--UMC Probable/Likely category and should never be undertaken
    solely for a project or without appropriate clinical justification.

## Why not Certain?

The limited information does not establish a definitive, specific
relationship or exclude all other explanations with sufficient
confidence. Therefore, the Certain category would not be justified from
this fictional summary.

## What additional information would improve the assessment?

-   Patient age and relevant medical history
-   Complete medication list and dates of use
-   Description and clinical evaluation of the rash
-   Relevant allergy history
-   Exact dates of onset, withdrawal, and improvement
-   Assessment of alternative causes and any supporting investigations
-   Relevant follow-up information

## Learning point

Causality assessment should use the whole case record. A temporal
association alone does not prove that a medicine caused an event, and
uncertainty should be documented honestly.
