# Causality Assessment in Pharmacovigilance

**Important:** The case below is hypothetical and is for learning
purposes only. It is not medical advice or a real patient report.

## Objective

To evaluate the likelihood of a relationship between a suspected
medicine and an adverse event by considering: - Time relationship
between medicine use and the event - Other possible explanations - What
happened after the medicine was stopped (dechallenge) - Whether
re-exposure information is available and appropriate

## Introduction

Pharmacovigilance is the science and activities related to detecting,
assessing, understanding, and preventing adverse effects or other
medicine-related problems. Causality assessment helps reviewers judge
how likely it is that a medicine contributed to a reported event. It
supports consistent review, but it does **not prove** causation.

## Method: WHO--UMC

The WHO--UMC system considers the clinical details and quality of
information in a report. Common categories include:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Category                            General interpretation
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Certain                             A plausible time relationship, no
                                      reasonable alternative explanation,
                                      a clinically/pharmacologically
                                      convincing event, and an
                                      appropriate response to withdrawal;
                                      rechallenge may be needed where
                                      relevant.

  Probable/Likely                     A reasonable time relationship,
                                      other causes are unlikely, and
                                      improvement after withdrawal is
                                      clinically reasonable. Rechallenge
                                      is not required.

  Possible                            A reasonable time relationship
                                      exists, but the event could also be
                                      explained by disease or another
                                      medicine, or withdrawal information
                                      is unclear.

  Unlikely                            Timing makes a relationship
                                      improbable and another explanation
                                      is more plausible.

  Conditional/Unclassified            More information is needed and is
                                      being sought or assessed.

  Unassessable/Unclassifiable         Information is insufficient or
                                      contradictory and cannot be
                                      supplemented or verified.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Use the full WHO--UMC guidance when conducting an actual assessment.
Categories should not be assigned from a single detail alone.

## Conclusion

Causality assessment is a structured way to evaluate suspected ADR
reports. The conclusion depends on the total evidence, including timing,
alternative explanations, and the course after withdrawal. A causality
category expresses the likelihood of a relationship; it does not
establish absolute proof.
